## Ammunition

**Ranged weapon users will be tracking ammunition in combat.** A character may carry up to 2 ammunition containers (e.g. bullet cases or quivers of arrows) on their person at any time, and they may freely access the ammunition from these containers to reload their weapons. A character may carry more containers in their backpack if they choose to, but they are not able to freely access the ammunition from them, and **swapping a container on their person with another one takes an Action in combat.** 

## Power Attacks

**Sharpshooter is banned in this campaign**. This is due to the prevalence of ranged combat in this setting, and the first two benefits of Sharpshooter removes two major aspects of tactical ranged combat in D&D: long range and cover. However, the third benefit (the -5 to attack for +10 to damage) is not an issue for ranged combat, so to allow players to keep that option for extra damage, I am implementing the following house rule. This does not require a feat and can work with any weapon attack (not just ranged weapon attacks).

**Before you make a weapon attack, you can choose to declare it to be a Power Attack. If you do, you take a -5 penalty to the attack roll, and if that attack hits, you add +10 to the attack's damage.**

## Attacked while Flying

Flying races are not restricted in this campaign. However, I will be using the following house rule: **If a creature that is flying takes damage from an attack, it must make a Strength Saving Throw or be knocked prone. The DC of the check is equal to 10 + half the damage of the attack.**

Keep in mind that when a creature is knocked prone while flying, it falls unless it has the ability to hover or it is being held aloft by magic.

## Casting in Armor

In addition to the normal spell casting rules for armor, I will be using the following house rule: **If you are casting a spell with a somatic component while wearing armor that has a Strength requirement and you do not meet the Strength requirement for that armor, you must make a Dexterity Check with a DC equal to the Strength requirement of the armor. If you succeed, you cast the spell normally, but if you fail, the spell fails and you still expend any spell slots and/or materials for that spell.**

See also [[Living Expenses]] for daily lifestyle costs and resting rules.
