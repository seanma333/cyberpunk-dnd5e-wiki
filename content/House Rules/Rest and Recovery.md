The following rules are about how resting works in this campaign. These are to reinforce how the cyberpunk world operates: the dichotomy of the rich and the poor.

## Lifestyles

While often ignored in D&D campaigns, daily living expenses are a huge part of living in a cyberpunk world: the poor struggle to survive while the rich thrive.

At the end of each day, you must pay for a lifestyle cost to have lived that lifestyle for the day. The lifestyle cost is a total cost which covers getting a room, food, and other lifestyle expenses. If these expenses are not paid, you will be sleeping on the street in Wretched conditions, and you are very likely to be robbed (if in a poorer district) or jailed (if in a richer district).

The lifestyles are, in order from cheapest to most expensive: **Wretched**, **Squalid**, **Poor**, **Modest**, **Comfortable**, **Wealthy**, and **Aristocratic**. Many of the rules in this section are dependent on lifestyle.

## Home Base

Lancers typically want to establish a **home base** within the city: a place that they can safely rest and recover without fear of being hunted or attacked. This can be set up by a fixer, or be within the territory of an allied faction upon agreement. The DM will tell the players when an area is fit to be a home base. Depending on the players' actions or how the story proceeds, an area may no longer be fit as a home base.

When resting within a home base, the players can live up to one level above the average lifestyle of the district that home base is in. When resting outside their home base, the players can live up to the average lifestyle of the district.

## Resting

The cyberpunk world is tough, and getting time to rest and recover is a luxury not afforded to everyone. As such, the following rules of resting are updated from the normal D&D 5th Edition rules.

### Long Rests

In a variation of the Gritty Realism rules of D&D 5th Edition, long rests require **3 days** in a **safe location**. The DM will inform the players when they are in a safe location for a long rest, though most times the players will want to establish a [[#Home Base|home base]], as a place for them to return to in order to recover safely.

Furthermore, in this campaign, **hit dice are completely recovered on a long rest**, as opposed to half of the hit dice in standard D&D 5th Edition rules.

### Partial Rests

Sometimes lancer missions can take multiple days, and everyone needs to sleep at the end of each day. Resting for one night in such a way results in a **partial rest**, a new type of rest for this campaign. A partial rest is like a long rest in a standard D&D game: a period of extended downtime of 8 hours where characters sleep for at least 6 hours and perform no more than 2 hours of light activity. However, *it is not a long rest*, and so the benefits of a long rest do not apply.

When you complete a partial rest, the following effects happen in order:

1. You gain all the benefits of a short rest, including spending hit dice to recover hit points.
2. You regain half your expended hit dice (rounded down).
3. If you have any levels of exhaustion, you can make a Constitution check. The DC of the check is the **Rest DC** of the character's lifestyle for the previous day, as outlined in the [[#Lifestyle Table]]. On a success, you may remove one level of exhaustion. This check cannot be modified by spells or other effects.
4. If you have the Spellcasting feature, you can regain some of your expended spell slots. The spell slots can have a combined level that is equal to or less than half your spellcaster level (rounded up), and none of the slots can be 6th level or higher.
5. If you have any features (not including Spellcasting) that are gained by class, race, feats, or [[System Updates/Cyberware/index|cyberware]] that recover after a long rest, you can recover up to half the expended uses of those features (rounded up). Cyberware that regains charges over a long rest regains half the rolled amount (rounded up).
6. You may roll for a Rest Point (see [[#Rest Points]] below).

If a character ends a 24-hour period without finishing a partial or a long rest, they must make a DC 10 Constitution saving throw or suffer one level of exhaustion. The DC increases by 5 for each consecutive 24-hour period without a partial or long rest. The DC resets to 10 when they finish a long rest (but not a partial rest).

### Rest Points

When taking a partial or long rest, you have a chance to gain **Rest Points**.

At the end of a day when taking a partial or a long rest, you may roll a d20. If the result is equal to or higher than the Rest DC of the character's lifestyle for the previous day, you may gain one Rest Point. A character can have a maximum of 3 rest points at any one time.

Whenever you make an attack roll, an ability check, or a saving throw, you can spend one rest point to roll an additional d20. You can choose to spend one of your rest points after you roll the die, but before the outcome is determined. You choose which of the d20s is used for the attack roll, ability check, or saving throw.

You can also spend one rest point when an attack roll is made against you. Roll a d20 and then choose whether the attack uses the attacker's roll or yours.

If more than one creature spends a rest point to influence the outcome of a roll, the points cancel each other out; no additional dice are rolled.

> [!note]
> Rest Points work exactly like luck points from the Lucky feat. Because of this, **the Lucky feat is banned in this campaign.**
