As one of the largest habitable areas left after the wars, [[Zeal City]]'s inhabitants are from all over Ryse. All of the D&D races are inhabitants of the city. The most common races are those found in the Player's Handbook, with humans being the most common at ~30% of the population. Few will question the presence of the common races, but it is rarer (though not unheard of) to see others. Certain races require special mention for their lore in this world.

## Dragonmarked

After the Mana Wars, each megacorporation has spent time and resources experimenting with meta-genetics research to create gifted individuals, either from birth or acquired at a later age. The results of their efforts are the Dragonmarked races. All dragonmarked individuals are related in some way to a megacorporation, and they bear their corresponding mark as a glowing tattoo-like symbol on their bodies. Anyone bearing a Dragonmark is assumed to be in some way affiliated with the corresponding megacorporation, even though the affiliation may be circumstantial (such as having the mark forcefully applied to them). There are dragonmarked individuals in the highest levels of each megacorporation.

See [[Setting and Lore/Megacorporations/index|Megacorporations]] for the affiliated dragonmarks.

## Fey Races

50 years ago, portals to the Feywild opened up around Ryse, leading to a migration of Fey races appearing around the world. One such portal is in the [[Selesnya District|Selesnya district]] of Zeal City. During the last few decades, Fey races have since made Zeal City their home, though anti-fey sentiment can be quite strong. The Fey Crossings are closed now, though once in a while, the Selesnya Fey Crossing has been known to shimmer during dusk.

Fey races are races whose creature type is Fey rather than Humanoid (e.g. Centaur, Changeling, Fairy, Satyr).

## Simic Hybrid

Some early meta-genetics research in Zeal University was looking into why the gift of magic only appeared among certain races, and attempts were made to research the genetics of animals and possible links to the humanoid meta-gene. The discoveries from this research became the basis for a project funded by multiple megacorporations to create hyper-evolved humanoids with animal traits, and the result was dubbed the Simic Hybrid, after the district where this research was primarily conducted.

## Warforged

During the Mana Wars, there was a need to create a special type of soldier to combat the magical forces of the Coalition, as their restless golems and constructs were wreaking havoc on the armies of the New World. The earliest versions of these soldiers were mindless automatons, and while they were effective, they lacked adaptability. However, an unexpected breakthrough came with a combination of magical engineering and artificial intelligence, producing what are now known as Warforged: constructed metal soldiers that had free will, as well as the ability to use magic. Most historians say that it was the mass construction of these sapient steel soldiers that turned the tide of the war.

Now, in the many years since the war ended, the warforged struggle to find purpose in the world after the wars they were built for. Many warforged embrace a concrete purpose and embrace this task as they once did war. However, there are warforged who delight in exploring their feelings, their freedom, and their relationships with others. Most warforged have no interest in religion, but some embrace faith and mysticism, seeking higher purpose and deeper meaning.
