Religion is not as ubiquitous in Ryse as it is in other D&D settings, but it still plays a part in many people's lives. For most, it is a source of shared community and ideals, with religious leaders being generally well-respected. However, corruption exists everywhere, and the houses of prayer are no exception.

There are a plethora of stories written throughout history about each of the deities, but because some stories directly contradict others, religious scholars have argued for generations about the "One Divine Truth". Regardless, it is well-known that there are those who become gifted with divine magic purely through prayer, becoming the Clerics and Paladins of this world.

Note: While there are some people who pray to multiple deities, it is well-known that the few individuals who become gifted through their prayers are all devoted to a single deity.

## Pantheon

The deities of Ryse do not have strict names. Instead, they have been referred to since ancient times by the names of their constellations. They each represent a singular Cleric domain, and unlike most pantheons, there is no alignment for the deities. Instead, actions based on each deity's ideals can be considered good or evil, depending on context. As examples, good followers of *The Trickster* have used deception to save lives, whereas evil followers of *The Diplomat* have created peace through tyrannical rule.
