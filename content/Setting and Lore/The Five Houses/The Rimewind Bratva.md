> **Territory:** The Coldworks

## History

The Rimewind Bratva was born in the gulags.

When the old Coalition held sway over **Kjeldova** — the frozen northern territories — they did what gifted ruling classes do: they found the most valuable thing in the ground and built a system to extract it. The residuum veins of Kjeldova ran deep, far beneath the permafrost, in tunnels only the underground peoples knew how to navigate. The **Stromgald Compact**, Kjeldova's ruling magical order, solved this problem with forced labor. Dark elves and duergar — already adapted to the deep places — were driven underground in chains, working the mines under magical compulsion while the surface world burned residuum to build its future.

What the Stromgald did not anticipate was what happens when you put the most resilient peoples in the world into the dark together and tell them they have nothing. They organized. They developed a code — the *Bratva* — that bound them across racial lines: dark elf to duergar, surface elf to human, anyone willing to live by the law of the tunnels. The code had one central tenet: *never serve those who would own you*. No cooperation with the Compact, no bowing to authority, no legitimate labor in a system built on your suffering.

When the [[Setting and Lore/index#Welcome to the New Age|Mana Wars]] came and the Stromgald Compact collapsed, the Bratva walked out of the mines — into a new world that turned out to be just another system built to exploit people. They adapted. Their skills translated perfectly.

## Leadership: Volk

**Volk** is the Pakhan of the Rimewind Bratva in [[Zeal City]] — and unlike most criminal leaders of his standing, he is not hidden. He walks the Coldworks openly. He sits at the bar at The Furnace most evenings. The people of the neighborhood know his face, know his name, and know exactly what he is. This is not carelessness. It is a statement: he does not hide because he does not need to. The openness is itself a kind of power.

A duergar of indeterminate age — old enough that some believe he came out of the actual gulags, though he does not confirm or deny this — he is covered in Vor tattoos that glow a faint, cold blue in the dark. Whether Volk is his birth name or a title that became a name so long ago nobody remembers the difference is not something anyone asks. He is not warm, not elegant, not grandmotherly. He is simply *iron*. The weight of what he has survived communicates everything a threat would, without the waste of words.

## Structure

Rank within the Bratva is demonstrated through **magical tattoos** — enchanted ink that records oaths, crimes, and allegiances in a language only other Vor can fully read. Forging them is nearly impossible. Removing them leaves traces that cannot be hidden.

- **Pakhan** — Supreme leader of the Bratva in a given territory. Commands through respect, not appointment.
- **Avtoritet** — Senior Vor who act as lieutenants and regional leaders.
- **Vor** — Full members. Bound by the code. Thieves-in-law.
- **Boyevik** — Soldiers. Not full Vor, but proven and trusted.
- **Shestyorka** — Associates and runners. The bottom of the hierarchy, often young, often desperate.

## Territory: The Coldworks

The Rimewind Bratva's home in [[Rakdos District|Rakdos]] is the **Coldworks** — a neighborhood on the district's southeastern edge, where the neon thins out into concrete. The architecture here is brutalist and functional: thick walls, small windows, buildings that were built to last rather than to impress. No decorative signage. No wasted space. Everything looks like it was designed by someone who had never once trusted that comfort would last.

The Bratva's anchor venue is **The Furnace** — a bar and meeting hall built into a decommissioned industrial substation. Hot, loud, and dim. Black iron fittings, low lighting, the distant smell of something burning. The tattooed Vor drink at the bar like any other regulars. The tattoos do all the talking.

## Rackets

- **Theft and Smuggling** — A direct inheritance from the gulag code, where taking from those who owned you was not a crime but a right. The Bratva runs some of the most sophisticated theft operations in the city, with particular expertise in hitting corporate targets.
- **Underground Networks** — The duergar and dark elf core maintain contacts in the undercity, the Metro tunnels, and supply chains that operate entirely out of [[Medani Security Group|Medani]]'s sight. If something needs to move through Zeal City without appearing on any record, the Bratva can make that happen.
- **Debt and Enforcement** — The Bratva runs a significant lending operation in the Coldworks and surrounding areas. The rates are fair by criminal standards. The consequences of default are not.
- **Mercenary Work** — The Boyevik are available for hire through intermediaries. Not as polished as other networks, but cheaper and willing to take jobs others won't.

## Reputation

The Rimewind Bratva is regarded as the most *ideologically coherent* of the Five Houses — which is either admirable or unsettling depending on your perspective. The code is real. A Vor who breaks it faces consequences from within the brotherhood before anyone outside has a chance to act.

Among the other Houses, the Bratva is respected but kept at distance. The [[The Lazuli Mafia|Lazuli]] find their directness useful and their politics tedious. The [[The Black Lotus Triad|Black Lotus Triad]] and the Bratva have a functional working relationship rooted in a shared history of being peoples the old world tried to grind up.

Among Zeal City's dark elf and duergar communities, the Bratva occupies a complicated space — protector, employer, and occasional predator, depending on the day. The gulag memory runs deep. Some see the Bratva as the inheritors of that survival. Others see them as the next system asking to own you.

The Vor themselves do not particularly care what anyone thinks. They have the code. Everything else is noise.
