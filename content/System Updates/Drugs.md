While the alchemists and apothecaries of old dealt with herbal remedies and potion crafting, modern advancements in chemistry have led to the creation of potent **drugs**. From the gang-infested streets of [[Gruul District|Gruul]], to the clubs of mob-ruled [[Rakdos District|Rakdos]], to the rich high rises of [[Orzhov District|Orzhov]], drugs are everywhere in [[Zeal City]], as well as a multitude of drug addicts.

Lancers in Zeal City are often hired on jobs dealing with drugs, whether it be to prevent the spread of drugs in an area or stealing experimental formulas from megacorps. Lancers can expect to encounter drugs multiple times during their career, whether from dealers in the streets or at rave parties. Should anyone wish to take drugs and experience their temporary potent effects, they will have an increasing risk of addiction. The following rules apply to characters who want to take drugs during a campaign.

## In General

For the purposes of the game rules, drugs are consumable items, and the minimum amount consumed is called a **dose**. Taking less than a dose of a drug has no effects, positive or negative. Consuming a dose of a drug takes an action, and grants temporary effects for a period of time, usually one hour.

However, drugs are addictive. While gaining the effects of multiple drugs might be tempting, taking too many in a short period can result in addictive side effects. See [[#Drug Dependency]] for how drug dependency works in the game.

## Street Drugs

The drugs listed here are commonly found on the streets, and are known by most of the city's denizens.

### Bliss

One of the most popular street narcotics, especially in impoverished areas, Bliss gives the user temporary amnesia, specifically blocking out their worst memories. This creates a state of euphoria which makes them more approachable and confident, but also increases their susceptibility to manipulation. Most forms of Bliss look like a dark purple liquid which can be injected.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Charisma checks and saving throws, but takes a -2 penalty on Intelligence checks and saving throws. In addition, the user has advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened. These effects last for 1 hour.

### Amp

A street drug commonly found at raves and clubs, Amp is a stimulant which keeps the user mentally alert and conscious even if they haven't slept. Amp comes in the form of a blue powder or pill, which can be consumed orally or nasally.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Constitution checks and saving throws, but a -2 penalty to Wisdom checks and saving throws. These effects last for 1 hour.

In addition, when consumed, the user has advantage on the next Constitution saving throw they make to avoid exhaustion for not attempting a [[Rest and Recovery#Partial Rests|partial or long rest]] within the next 24 hours, though the exhaustion DC increases as normal.

### Grind

Grind is a muscle enhancement drug, which suppresses the body's natural limiters for muscle use. This makes the user stronger, but also makes them more vulnerable to bodily damage. This drug was once used by athletes, but has since been banned for its overuse and negative addictive qualities. Grind normally takes the form of a red solution that can be injected.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Strength checks and saving throws, but a -2 penalty to Constitution checks and saving throws. In addition, the user's base carrying capacity is increased by 20 pounds while the drug is active. These effects last for 1 hour.

### Jazz

A drug used by enforcers and mafia soldiers, Jazz temporarily increases a user's reaction speed, but often makes the user jittery in social situations. Jazz comes in the form of an arm patch.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Dexterity checks and saving throws, but a -2 penalty to Charisma checks and saving throws. While the drug is active, the user also gains a +1d4 bonus to initiative checks. The drug's effects last for 1 hour.

### Sharp

Sharp is a stimulant that enhances the user's senses. However, the effect is so potent that the user can experience sensory overload, which impedes their actual physical reaction speed. This drug was originally developed for treasure hunters that would dive into the ruins of the old world, but nowadays it's used more as a psychoactive street drug. Its form is of a pale orange powder and is normally taken in pill form.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Wisdom checks and saving throws, but receives a -2 penalty to Dexterity checks and saving throws. In addition, the user gains a +1d4 bonus on Wisdom (Perception) and Intelligence (Investigation) checks. These effects last for 1 hour.

### Muse

A hallucinogen and stimulant that is known to open one's mind, allowing the user to perfectly recall long forgotten memories and put together connections. However, this also has the side effect of weakening the body's muscles, and memories suppressed by other drugs like Bliss remain inaccessible. Muse is commonly sold in the form of a pink dissolvable tablet.

When consumed, the user gains a +2 bonus to Intelligence checks and saving throws, but receives a -2 penalty to Strength checks and saving throws. In addition, while the drug is active, the user can accurately recall anything they have seen or heard within the past month, and gets a +1d4 bonus to Wisdom (Insight) checks. These effects last for 1 hour.

## Drug Dependency

Each character has a **Dependency Number**, which represents their level of addiction to drugs. A character that hasn't taken drugs before is clean and has a dependency number of 0, which is the minimum dependency number. This single number is tracked separately for each character, and any and all drugs can affect this number.

The more dependent a character is on drugs, the more doses it requires for them to feel the effects. **In order to gain the effects of a drug, the character must take a number of doses equal to or greater than their dependency number.** So, a character with a dependency number of 2 requires at least two doses of a single drug to feel the effects of that drug.

At the end of a 24-hour day, the following occurs.

### Addiction

Addiction is a disease of both body and mind. At the end of a 24-hour day, if a character has consumed at least one dose of drugs, they must make both a **Constitution and a Wisdom saving throw**. The DC for both saving throws is **10 + the character's dependency number + the number of drug doses taken during the last 24 hours**. If they succeed at both saving throws, their Dependency Number remains unchanged. If they succeed at one saving throw and fail the other, their Dependency Number **increases by half the number of doses taken (rounded down)**. If they fail *both* saving throws, their Dependency Number **increases by the number of doses taken**.

### Withdrawal

Drugs taken carefully in moderation present little risk of side effects. However, when a person becomes more dependent on drugs, they will start to feel **withdrawal** if they have not gotten their fix. The number of doses of drugs required for a character to not face withdrawal at the end of a 24-hour day is called the **fix number**, and is equal to **half the character's dependency number (rounded down)**. If a character does not satisfy their fix number at the end of the 24 hours, they gain **one level of exhaustion**. If their dependency number is 5 or greater, they instead gain **two levels of exhaustion**.

### Cold Turkey

There is only one way to lower a character's dependency number, and that is for a character to take 0 drug doses in a day, in a process colloquially known as **cold turkey**. During the end of the day, if a character has not taken *any* drugs in the last 24 hours, they face the normal withdrawal effects if their fix number is greater than zero, but their Dependency Number is reduced by 1, to a minimum of 0.

### Overdosing

Unless otherwise stated, the effects of drugs will stack with each other if their effect periods overlap. However, this can lead to **overdosing**. If a character consumes a dose of a drug while still under the effects of at least one drug, the player must make an **overdosing saving throw**. This is a Constitution saving throw with a DC equal to 10 + the number of doses of drugs taken within the last hour, including the one that triggered the saving throw. On a failure, the character will feel the effects of the drug, if any, but then the character immediately takes 1 level of exhaustion. On a natural 1, the character instead takes 3 levels of exhaustion.
